Redesign the Meetrao marketing website from the ground up:

**Website:** https://meetrao.com/

I want a major improvement in **content, positioning, layout, visual storytelling, animation, and conversion**, while keeping Meetrao's existing brand identity and **current color scheme**.

## Product Context

Meetrao is a **completely free meeting and appointment scheduling SaaS**.

Core flow:

**Connect Google Calendar → Set availability → Share booking link → Guest chooses a time → Meeting is booked**

Key capabilities include:
- Google Calendar integration
- Calendar conflict checking
- Availability rules
- Timezone handling
- Google Meet link creation
- Working-hour protection
- Simple booking links

Do not invent features or capabilities.

---

## Core Positioning

The main message should be:

# Stop asking “What time works for you?”

Supporting copy:

**Meetrao turns your availability into one simple booking link, so clients, customers, and teammates can choose a time that works — without the back-and-forth.**

Strongly emphasize:

# Meeting scheduling. Completely free.

Primary CTA:

**Create your free booking link →**

Secondary CTA:

**See how it works**

The website should make users think:

**“Why am I still scheduling meetings manually when I can do this for free?”**

Position Meetrao as a **premium product that happens to be free**, not as a cheap/free-looking tool.

Structure the page as a continuous narrative:

### 1. Hero

**Stop asking “What time works for you?”**

- Calendar
- Available times
- Timezone

### 2. The Problem

> “I'm busy Tuesday. What about Wednesday?”  
> “Wednesday at 3?”  
> “Which timezone?”  
> “Can we do Thursday instead?”

**5 messages later…**

- Back-and-forth

- Calendar conflicts

- Wasted time

# You don't need another scheduling conversation.

### 3. Why Scheduling Matters

Create a credible research-backed section showing that scheduling and appointment management have real operational impact.

Use **real, verifiable statistics**, not invented numbers.

Potential research:
- A systematic review of 105 studies found an average appointment no-show rate of approximately 23%.
- Frontiers in Digital Health research found lower no-show rates for online-booked appointments compared with offline bookings in one ophthalmology practice.

Include source attribution.
